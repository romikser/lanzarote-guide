LANZAROTE

A volcanic island with no high-rises, almost no billboard advertising, and a single artist who shaped everything. Four days is enough to see the real thing.

The island

More than
a beach resort

Lanzarote is the only island in the Canaries where a single person's aesthetic decisions are still visibly intact. César Manrique — painter, sculptor, architect — returned from New York in 1966 and spent the next three decades fighting to keep the island free of the tourist architecture that swallowed Tenerife and Gran Canaria. No high-rises above the palm trees. No billboards. Buildings in white and black and green, the traditional Canarian palette. The island held. Barely, but it held.

What's left is a genuinely strange place: volcanic in a way that makes Iceland feel familiar, yet entirely warm and Spanish. The landscape shifts from lunar lava fields at Timanfaya to the green crater-vineyard valleys of La Geria to the wild surf beach at Famara. Manrique's fingerprints are everywhere — the Jameos del Agua built inside a lava tube, the Mirador del Río carved into a clifftop, the Cactus Garden in a former quarry. You have to see them. Even if you're suspicious of designed attractions, these work.

Long weekend guide · Thu–Mon · 2026
Practical

Fly into Arrecife (ACE). Rent a car on arrival — the island is compact but public transport is poor outside Arrecife. The whole island is 60km by 25km; you can reach anything in under an hour. August is hot (28–32°C) but the northeast Trade winds keep it from being oppressive. Book Timanfaya in advance — entry is timed and sells out.

North

Volcano · Photography · Hike
Timanfaya
The Fire Mountains. A national park of lava fields and cinder cones formed between 1730–36. El Diablo restaurant cooks over actual geothermal heat. Go early.
→
Wine · History · Village
Wine Country
La Geria valley: vines planted in individual volcanic craters (socos) to trap moisture. El Grifo winery, founded 1775. Teguise, the old capital with cobbled streets.
