TIMANFAYA

Six years of eruptions between 1730 and 1736 buried a quarter of the island under lava. What's left is one of the most alien landscapes in Europe. Go early, before the coaches arrive.

Between 1730 and 1736, a series of volcanic eruptions reshaped the southwest of Lanzarote in a way that modern geology still studies. Over six years, fissure after fissure opened across what had been farmland and villages. Twenty-two villages were buried. The lava flows reached the sea. When it finally stopped, a quarter of the island's total surface area was under new volcanic rock. A further eruption in 1824 added more.

The result is Timanfaya National Park: 51km² of lava fields, cinder cones, and volcanic craters that remain largely uninhabited and almost entirely unmodified. The rock is young enough that very little vegetation has taken hold — just the hardiest succulents and the occasional fig tree that sends roots down through cracks in the lava. From the road, it looks like another planet.

The geothermal heat is still active at Islote de Hilario: 300°C at 13m depth, 100°C at 6m, 400°C at the surface in some spots. The demonstrations (pouring water into a crack, watching it geyser; lowering a bush toward a vent and watching it combust) are genuinely startling. El Diablo restaurant, designed by Manrique, uses this geothermal heat to grill food over volcanic vents. Book it if you can — it's a serious experience, not a gimmick.

National Park
Timanfaya Route de los Volcanes
Entrance via Yaiza (south) or Mancha Blanca (north)
The 40-minute bus tour of the volcanic interior — the only way to see the heart of the park (private vehicles are prohibited beyond the car park). You board a coach at Islote de Hilario and are driven through the lava fields, between cinder cones, along routes that would otherwise be inaccessible. Commentary in multiple languages. The landscape is extraordinary and the scale of the 1730–36 event becomes clear when you're inside it. Book timed entry online well in advance in August. Entry €14. Go first slot of the day.

National Park · Geology
Islote de Hilario
Centre of Timanfaya park
The geothermal demonstration area at the park's centre. Rangers pour water into a bore hole — it geysers out as steam within seconds (100°C at 6m depth). They lower a bundle of dry brushwood to a vent at close range and it combusts. Unsettling in the best way. The ground temperature a short distance from the paths is 400°C at the surface in places. Manrique's El Diablo restaurant is here. The camel rides around the park perimeter are optional (skip them).

Food · Manrique
El Diablo Restaurant
Islote de Hilario, Timanfaya
Manrique-designed circular restaurant at the peak of Islote de Hilario. The kitchen uses a volcanic grill: food is cooked on a metal grate positioned above a natural geothermal vent. Not a theatrical gimmick — the heat is real and consistent, producing dry-heat cooking that works particularly well with chicken and fish. Panoramic views of the lava field. More expensive than Arrecife restaurants but the experience is worth it once. Book ahead online — it fills quickly. Lunch only.

Hiking · Permit Required
Caldera Blanca Hike
Outside the national park boundary, west of Mancha Blanca
A 9km circular hike to the rim of Caldera Blanca — a 458m volcanic crater with a 1.2km diameter. This is outside the restricted national park zone so no permit is required (unlike the guided walks inside the park itself). The terrain is lava and cinders — proper footwear essential. The views from the rim into the caldera and back across the lava fields toward Timanfaya are exceptional. Start early to avoid the heat. No shade at all. Two to three hours.

Beach · Swimming
Playa del Papagayo
South coast, near Playa Blanca
The clearest water on the island — a protected cove with white sand and turquoise water that would look more at home in the Maldives than the Canaries. €3 per car for the access road. Multiple coves within the Papagayo reserve; the furthest ones (from the car park) are quieter and worth the walk. Bring your own food and water — the bar facilities are limited. Snorkelling is excellent off the rocks. Combine with Timanfaya morning → Papagayo afternoon.

🌊
Beach · Relaxed
El Golfo
West coast, north of Playa Blanca
A volcanic crater partially collapsed into the sea — one half is a wall of green-tinged volcanic rock, the other is the Pacific-looking lagoon El Charco de los Clicos (the green lagoon), coloured by algae and minerals. The beach is black volcanic sand. The cluster of seafood restaurants on the cliff above the lagoon is the best place for a late lunch after Timanfaya — fresh fish, cold Canarian wine, views over the crater. Not worth a separate trip but excellent combined with the park visit.

Photography

What to
shoot

Timanfaya is a photographer's location that requires early arrival — by 11am the coach groups arrive and the light has hardened. First slot of the day (9am) gives you the best conditions: the low angle light catches the texture of the lava, the colours in the volcanic rock range from rust to black to an almost luminous orange in the right light. The bus tour doesn't stop — photograph through the windows. Best non-moving shots are at Islote de Hilario before and after the tour.
