La Geria · El Grifo · Teguise

WINECOUNTRY

Vines growing in volcanic craters, sheltered by low stone walls, drawing moisture from the lava itself. El Grifo has been making wine here since 1775. The oldest winery in the Canary Islands.

Malvasía
The grape variety that defines La Geria — both dry and sweet styles

After the 1730–36 eruptions buried the most fertile farmland on the island, the farmers who returned discovered something counterintuitive: the volcanic lapilli (the layer of coarse black volcanic grit covering the landscape) was exceptional for agriculture. Porous enough to absorb moisture from sea fog and dew; stable enough to retain warmth; mineral-rich in ways the old soil wasn't.

The result is La Geria: a valley in the centre of the island where vine growers developed a system found nowhere else in the world. Each vine is planted at the bottom of a hand-dug crater (a soco) in the lapilli — some of these craters are two metres deep and four metres wide. Low semicircular stone walls (zocos) protect the plants from the Trade winds on the windward side. The result, from above, is one of the strangest agricultural landscapes you'll see: a black field covered in thousands of individual stone crescents, each sheltering a single vine.

The wine, mostly Malvasía Volcánica, is excellent — dry whites with mineral depth from the lava soil, semi-sweet versions with more body. Teguise, 20 minutes north, was the island's capital for four centuries before Arrecife. The cobbled streets, the castle on the hill, and the Saturday antiques market reward an afternoon.

Wine · Founded 1775
Bodega El Grifo
La Geria valley, near Masdache
The oldest winery in the Canary Islands, operating continuously since 1775. The self-guided audio tour (€15, 45 minutes) covers the history, the winemaking process, and the extraordinary lapilli landscape. Includes a 2-wine tasting — get the Malvasía Volcánica Seco (dry) and the Malvasía Semi Dulce. The museum section is genuinely good: antique presses, the evolution of the soco system, maps of the lava coverage. Book online in advance. The winery shop sells the full range and ships internationally.

Wine · Estate
Bodega La Geria
La Geria valley, Uga road
The estate with the most dramatic visual setting in La Geria — the bodega sits in the middle of the soco vineyard, surrounded by the crater-vine landscape. Tastings run throughout the day without reservation: 3 wines, roughly €8. The Malvasía dry is excellent, the rosé is approachable, the red is serviceable. The terrace is the point — you sit among the socos with the volcanic field spreading out behind the winery. Good if you want a more relaxed, less structured experience than El Grifo.

Village · History
Teguise
North-centre of the island, 20km from Arrecife
Lanzarote's capital for four centuries (1402–1852). Cobbled streets, whitewashed buildings with dark volcanic doorways and window frames, a castle on the hill (Castillo de Santa Bárbara), and a Sunday antiques market that spills across the main square. The town is compact and walkable in 45 minutes — the church of Nuestra Señora de Guadalupe dominates the main plaza. Better on a Sunday (market day) than mid-week when some of the restaurants are closed. The palace (Palacio del Marqués) is the grandest building; the art gallery inside is sometimes open.

Village · Food
Palacio Ico Restaurant
Teguise old town
In the restored Palacio Ico, one of Teguise's historic buildings. Michelin Guide 2025 listed (Michelin Recommendation) and 1 Sol Repsol 2025. The cooking is modern Canarian — takes the island's ingredients seriously, uses Malvasía in some dishes, good presentation without being precious. Best for a longer lunch after the morning market and winery. The courtyard setting inside the palace is handsome. Book ahead for Saturday lunch in August.

Landscape · Drive
La Geria Valley Drive
LZ-30 road between Uga and Masdache
The LZ-30 cuts through the heart of La Geria — this is the road to drive slowly and stop along. The soco vineyard system is most visible from the road itself: thousands of crescent-shaped stone walls, each sheltering a single vine in its volcanic crater. Pull over anywhere. There are no fences keeping you back. Walk into the vineyard between the socos and understand the scale. There are small signs identifying grape varieties. Best in morning light when the low sun catches the texture of the black lapilli against the stone walls. 15km from Uga to Masdache; take an hour.

Wine · Natural
Stratvs Bodega
La Geria
The smaller, more modern operation in La Geria — focused on lower intervention winemaking and organic farming of the socos. Tastings by appointment. Worth contacting in advance if you want a deeper wine experience than the bigger bodegas offer — smaller production, more attention per visitor. The Listán Blanco and the orange wine are interesting. Less formal than El Grifo, more personal. If you're serious about wine, add this.
