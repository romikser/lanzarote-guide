Haría · Famara · Jameos · Mirador

THENORTH

The most interesting part of the island. Manrique's two great works, the valley of palms, and the wildest beach in the Canaries — all in one day if you start early enough.

The north of Lanzarote is where the density of worthwhile things reaches its peak. Within a roughly 20km radius you have: César Manrique's Jameos del Agua (an underground concert hall and garden built inside a lava tube), the Mirador del Río (a clifftop observatory embedded in the rock), the village of Haría in its palm valley, and Famara beach.

The northeast corner — the Corona volcano area — produced the longest known lava tube in the world: 7km, running from the volcano all the way under the sea to La Graciosa. Part of that tube is accessible at Jameos del Agua, where Manrique built a concert hall, a saltwater lake (home to a species of blind albino crab found nowhere else on earth), and a garden of such obsessive care that it feels more like an installation than a park. It's extraordinary. Not in a "grand tourist attraction" way — in a "someone brilliant spent years on this" way.

Famara is the opposite: entirely natural, almost entirely empty of facilities. A long arc of sand with the Risco de Famara cliffs at 600m behind it. Surf school town (Caleta de Famara) at one end. The light in the last 90 minutes before sunset is exceptional for photography.

Manrique · Culture
Jameos del Agua
Near Arrieta, northeast coast
Manrique's most ambitious work. A section of the 7km Corona lava tube, entered via a staircase into volcanic rock. At the bottom: a saltwater lake inhabited by Munidopsis polymorpha — albino blind crabs unique to this ecosystem, washed in from the Atlantic through an underwater connection. Beyond the lake: a concert hall that seats 600, still used for performances. Beyond that: a terraced garden of extreme intentionality. The whole complex took years to build and shows. Entry €12. Allow 75–90 minutes.

Manrique · Viewpoint
Mirador del Río
Cliffs above Órzola, northwest tip
A viewpoint embedded into the clifftop at 479m, looking over the El Río strait to La Graciosa island. Manrique designed it to be invisible from below — from outside, it's just rock. Inside: a curved white café-bar with panoramic windows. The view across the water to La Graciosa is one of the most cinematic things on the island. Entry €5. The café is fine for a coffee. 30–40 minutes is enough. Best in morning light or late afternoon. Come immediately after Jameos del Agua — they're 15 minutes apart.

Manrique · Garden
Jardín de Cactus
Guatiza, east coast
Manrique's last public project — 1,500 cacti from 450 species arranged in a former quarry, with a restored windmill on the rim. Manrique died in a car accident in 1992 shortly after its completion. The obsessive care visible in Jameos del Agua is here too, at a more focused scale. Entry €6. 45 minutes. On the road between Arrecife and the north — worth doing on the way through rather than making a separate trip.

Nature · Photography
Playa de Famara
West coast, below Risco de Famara cliffs
A long wild beach facing southwest toward La Graciosa, backed by 600m cliffs that make it feel like somewhere in Iceland rather than the Canaries. Surf school town (Caleta de Famara) at the north end — small, local, not much else. The beach gets powerful waves in August; swimming is possible but check conditions. Photography is the main reason to come: the cliffs, the light, the silhouettes of surfers. Come 90 minutes before sunset and stay until dark. Take a jacket — the Trade winds are consistent here.

Village · Market
Haría
Valley of a Thousand Palms, north interior
A white village in a lush valley of palm trees — the contrast with the volcanic landscape you've been driving through is startling. Manrique's final home is here, now a museum (Fundación César Manrique Haría). The Saturday artisan market runs 9am–2pm and is one of the most genuine on the island: Canarian music, local produce, ceramics, textiles. Not touristy in a performed way. Pull up a chair at a bar and watch. For lunch: any of the local bars doing menu del día around the main square.

Food · Seafood
Restaurante El Amanecer
Arrieta (fishing village, east coast)
Arrieta is a small fishing village on the east coast, close to Jameos del Agua. Stop here for lunch — several waterfront restaurants serve the catch of the day. El Amanecer and its neighbours do grilled fish, fried limpets (lapas a la plancha), and papas. Not destination dining but genuinely fresh and the village itself is quiet and real. Limpets grilled with butter and lemon on the terrace looking at fishing boats.

Photography

What to
shoot

The north is the most photographically rich part of the island. Famara at sunset is the single best shot. Jameos del Agua requires permission for any commercial photography but personal cameras are fine — the garden section has extraordinary light. Mirador del Río is best in morning light when the sea colour is deepest. The drive along the northeast coast between Arrieta and Órzola gives volcanic cliffs meeting black sand — worth stopping repeatedly.
